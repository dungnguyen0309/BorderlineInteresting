    Help
    Turn and zoom
    Drag the globe, and scroll or pinch to zoom. With the keyboard, select the globe, then use the arrow keys and + or −.
    Find
    Search by name, former name (Burma) or ISO code (VNM). Press or / to jump to the search box.
    Open
    Select a country or marker on the globe. Enter opens the one in the middle; Esc closes the panel.
    Rank
    Pick a figure under Colour by figure, then Show as list. A rank in a country's panel opens the same list, on any map.
    Compare
    Open a country, choose Compare, then pick a second country on the globe or in search.
    True size
    Pins a country's outline to the middle of the view at its real size while you turn the globe under it.
    Strategic lines
    Lines navies plan around, such as the first island chain off China and the GIUK gap in the North Atlantic.
    Contested areas
    Hatched land held by one government and claimed by another.
    Hot spots
    The switch at the top (the eye button on phones) hides the markers, lines and hatched areas, and brings back the same ones. Places has a tick for each layer.
    Sources

    Countries and figures

    • Borders and coastlines: Natural Earth 1:50m, via world-atlas. They show who controls an area on the ground and take no side on rival claims.
    • Country figures and income groups: World Bank, World Development Indicators (CC BY 4.0), latest year since 2015. Trends since 1976, age pyramids, economy and electricity mixes, forest cover and CO₂ come from the same source. Data built .
    • Codes, names, currencies, languages, capitals, areas and landlocked status: mledoze/countries (ODbL) and the World Bank.
    • Country overviews, leaders and forms of government: Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0), mostly from each country's infobox, with gaps filled from Wikidata (CC0), which also gives the first-level divisions.
    • Religion and ethnic group shares: CIA World Factbook (public domain), with each estimate's year; each country uses its own categories.
    • Adult height: NCD-RisC, A century of trends in adult human height (eLife 2016, CC BY 4.0). Modelled mean height at 18 for people born 1896 to 1996, every second year, in cm with the 95% credible interval. These are not current measurements: the latest cohort turned 18 in 2014. Only 72 of the 200 countries have measurements of people born in 1920 or earlier, and 134 of people born 1921 to 1945; elsewhere the early values come mostly from the model. NCD-RisC now lists this adult release as archived; its newer one (Lancet 2020) covers ages 5 to 19 only.
    • Blood groups: each country's own survey, as compiled in Wikipedia, Blood type distribution by country (CC BY-SA 4.0). Shown only for countries whose figures were checked against the publisher's own page.
    • Flags and coats of arms: Wikimedia Commons via Wikidata (P41, P94); each image shows its author and licence when opened.
